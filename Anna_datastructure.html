1 Introduction

The dataset GlobalGeoTree is a 6 million lines long dataset. It contains various informations about trees, collected by diverse groups all across the world, like the specie, taxonomic group and geographical location of the tree. Some environmental informations are also contained in this dataset, such as the annual mean temperature.

7 Species observed in this dataset

What are the different tree families referenced in this dataset?

Definition of each tree category:

  • Evergreen Needleleaf: An evergreen needleleaf refers to woody vegetation that retains needle-like or scale-like foliage year-round

  • Evergreen Broadleaf: An evergreen broadleaf is a plant or tree that has wide, flat leaves (rather than needles or scales) and retains its foliage year-round

  • Deciduous Broadleaf: a diverse group of flowering plants (angiosperms) characterized by flat leaves that are shed annually before winter (Oak, Maple, Beech, Birch, and Poplar/Aspen)

  • Deciduous Needleleaf : a unique group of conifers that, unlike their evergreen counterparts, shed all their needles annually in the autumn (rare trees)

We see here that a very important part of the observations in this dataset are deciduous broedleaf trees. It makes sense because most of the tree species spread across the world are part of this family. On the countrary, Deciduous needleleaf are rare and located in much more restrained areas.

What are the different taxonomic families represented in this dataset and in which proportion are they represented?

Again, here we see that the most represented taxonomic families in this dataset are also the most represented ones across the world, especially in North-America, Europe and the north of Asia, where most of the data come from. We can then consider that the data contained in this dataset are quite representative of the real worldwide tree repartition.

9 Geographical observations of species

How does the latitude influences the different species observed? (Lattitude and specific richness)

Species richness: simple count of the number of different species present in a specific biological community, landscape, or region Much more important in the north hemisphere than in the south hemisphere more important in tempered areas, much lower at the equador and near the poles.

We see here that the maximum specie richness are very close in the two hemisphere, but that the repartition is broader in the norther hemisphere than in the southern hemisphere (it is also because trees grow at higher latitudes in the norther hemisphere than in the southern hemisphere). Similar environmental conditions can be found in the two hemispheres but they are not spread following the same pattern.

How does the longitude influences the diffrent species observed (longitude and specifi richness)?

We see here that areas with a lot of specific richness correspond to the continental areas, but not much more can be taken out of this graph.

What is the local specific richness across the world?

We see here that, in the areas presenting observations, the specie richness does not present too important variations and is homogenous in most areas. By crossing this with the previous results, we can think that the specie richness does not variates but that the species involved do variate across the world.

How are the different tree species distributed depending on the latitude? Here, we only check for the most represented species.

With this figure, we see that among the most represented species in this dataset, they present different world repartitions. Most of them are mostly present in the norther hemisphere, but some are also present in the southern hemisphere (such as Dombeya acrifolia), and some are almost evenly present in the two hemispheres, such as Cornus acuminata.

14 Geogrpahic repartition of the data

We can see on this map that the observations cover almost the whole US territory, but that areas are missing data, namely in the center of the US and in Alaska. This is coherent since these areas are less populated than the coastal areas. However, there areas are also those presenting the most trees in the US, especially in the national parks zones. The conclusions drawned from the othe observations would then have to be put back into context.

Specie Richness: - Depending on the latitude:

Here, we see that more trees are observed in the northern part of the US (it is also the largest part if the US, while the south is restrained). However, this also corresponds to the location of national parks that are known to be a place where many species are protected. The specie richness is then higher than in other places. These places are also known to be more humid and colder than the southern parts of the US, which is a much more welcoming environment for trees. These hypothesis would have to be compared with real humidity and temperature data.

Specie richness depending on the longitude:

Once again, we see that more species have been observed in the eastern part of the US, where more observations have been made. However, even if less observations have been made in the central part of the US these observations still show various species, which results in a lower specie richness but still present (between -90 and -120°).

Local specie richness across the US territory:

As we said before, the specie richness is much more important near the coasts (where more observations have been made). Still, a specie richness superior to 150 is still considered as a good value, and is one of the characteristic of a sane ecosystem.
