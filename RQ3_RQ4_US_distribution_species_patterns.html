United States Tree Observations and Species Range Patterns

A focused GlobalGeoTree analysis with regional context

Research Focus

This report focuses on the United States while keeping a short regional context.

RQ3 - Regional and Country-Level Distribution
How are tree observations and recorded species distributed in the United States compared with major reference regions and countries?

RQ4 - Species Range and Category Patterns
Do observations of the same species, or species in the same Rare/Common/Frequent category, tend to cluster in similar geographic areas?

The report is intentionally selective. It uses fewer figures than the exploratory version so that each visualization has a clear role in the argument.

Regional Context

Interpretation. The United States is the main driver of the North American subset. Comparisons with Germany and France should therefore be interpreted as comparisons of dataset coverage and recording intensity, not only as biological richness.

United States Hotspots

Interpretation. US observations are not evenly distributed. The strongest concentrations are visible in the eastern US, West Coast, Florida, the Pacific Northwest, and parts of the Great Lakes region. Sparse areas may represent fewer observations, lower platform use, lower population density, access limitations, or genuinely different tree distributions.

Most Recorded US Species

Interpretation. The most recorded taxa are not necessarily the most abundant trees in the United States. They are the most recorded taxa in this dataset.

Species Range Patterns

Interpretation. Some species occupy many spatial grid cells, while others are tightly localized. However, a localized pattern can reflect true range limits, low observation effort, or the way a species entered GlobalGeoTree.

Rare, Common, and Frequent Categories

Interpretation. Category results apply only to species matched to a category label. In this run, the US has 711,966 observations and 390 species with known categories. The category analysis should therefore be treated as a categorized subset, not the full US dataset.

Overall Interpretation

The United States is the dominant part of the North American subset in GlobalGeoTree. Within the US, observations are spatially concentrated rather than evenly distributed, with strong hotspots along the coasts and in the eastern half of the country. Species also differ strongly in spatial spread: some are widespread across many grid cells, while others appear localized. Category patterns suggest that Frequent species tend to be more spatially widespread than Rare species, but this conclusion depends on the matched category subset and should not be generalized to all US species without checking category coverage.
